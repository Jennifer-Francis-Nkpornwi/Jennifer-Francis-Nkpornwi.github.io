Online Bakery App: Business Analysis Case Study

Defining the requirements behind a seamless online ordering experience — from browsing to checkout — for a bakery going digital.

RoleBusiness Analyst
ToolsBusiness Analysis, User-Centric Design, Requirements Documentation
TypePersonal project
SourceView on GitHub ↗

The business question

A bakery moving from in-store-only sales to an online ordering app needs more than a shopping cart bolted onto a website. What does a customer actually need to see to trust a same-day bakery order online — freshness, delivery windows, product availability — and where does the ordering flow have to be simplest to avoid cart abandonment?

My approach

I approached this as a user-centric requirements exercise: define who's ordering, what they need to feel confident before checkout, and what the business needs operationally on the other end to fulfil same-day bakery orders reliably.

  • Defined user requirements around product freshness, availability, and delivery timing
  • Documented the end-to-end ordering flow from browse to checkout to delivery confirmation
  • Identified operational requirements the business needed to fulfil orders reliably same-day
  • Produced a business-analysis case study translating those requirements into a build-ready brief

The case study

The project cover framing the bakery's move from in-store-only to a full online ordering experience.

What it enabled

Treating "freshness confidence" as a first-class requirement — not just a UI nice-to-have — is what separates this from a generic e-commerce checkout brief. For a same-day, perishable product, the business analysis has to work backward from operational fulfilment constraints just as much as forward from what the customer wants to see, and that two-directional requirements gathering is the core BA skill this project exercises.
